Free, open-source, client-side Minecraft mod

Keep the particles you want. Hide the ones you don't.

Particle Filter shows you exactly which particles are spawning around you, vanilla or modded, without needing to know their names. Then hide each one everywhere, only inside areas you choose, or only when specific blocks break. Everything else keeps its particles.

For when you want some particles gone, not all of them

The usual fix for too many particles is Minecraft's Particles: Minimal setting, which strips almost all of them from the whole game. Mods such as Particle Core and Sodium Extra let you turn individual particle types off globally. Particle Filter covers the cases those leave awkward:

SCANYou don't know what the particle is called

Large modpacks spawn hundreds of effects. Point at where they're coming from, press Scan, and get a list of particle IDs with how often each one appeared. Pick the one you want gone.

ZONESYou only want it gone in one place

Hide a particle inside a box you place around a farm, machine room, build or base, and keep it everywhere else in the world.

BLOCKSOnly certain blocks are the problem

Stop the breaking, mining and block-dust particles of chosen blocks, a whole mod's blocks, or a block tag, while other blocks still crumble normally.

How it works

Open the menu with /particles (or a keybind, or the Config button in the Mods list). The game keeps running behind it, so you can watch the result as you go.

  1. Scan nearby particles

    Look at where the particles come from, or stand near them, and press Scan. For 5 seconds it records every particle spawned within 6 blocks and lists them by ID, such as minecraft:flame or ae2:energy_fx, with counts.

  2. Choose what to hide

    Flip Everywhere or Here next to each particle you don't want. The Hidden page keeps one list of every rule and where it applies, with one-click unhide.

  3. Hide it everywhere, or just here

    Here adds the particle to the zone around the scanned spot, creating a 9×5×9 zone if there isn't one. Resize and move it while the outline updates live in the world.

What people use it for

It's a specialised tool. If the vanilla Particles setting already does what you want, you don't need it. It helps when particles from one source get in the way:

Busy bases, farms and automation

Growth accelerators, crop and mob farms, block breakers and other setups that throw particles nonstop. Hide them around the base and keep them elsewhere.

Particle lag

Where particles pile up into thousands, hidden ones are never created, which can win back frame rate. How much depends on your setup; see the FPS question.

Block-breaking debris

Quarries, harvesters and ores that burst into debris every time they're broken, by block, by mod (geore:*) or by tag (#c:ores).

Builds and atmosphere

Clear an effect out of one room or structure while weather, ambience and mod effects stay intact across the rest of the world.

Screenshots, recording and streaming

Clean up the view around a build or machine before you record, then switch every rule off with one key to show the original.

One annoying mod effect

Remove a single particle a mod adds, or effects that are distracting or uncomfortable to look at, without uninstalling the mod.

Features

The essentials are below. The README documents every command, keybind and setting.

Zones

Zones are named boxes with a position (center X/Z, bottom Y) and a size from 1 to 256 blocks per side. Each one hides its own list of particles, and only inside its box.

  • Edit in a side panel while the outline moves live in the world
  • Type exact values, use −/+ buttons, or snap to yourself or your crosshair
  • Outlines can stay visible while you play
  • Zones belong to one server (or singleplayer world) and dimension

Particle scanner

Finds particles by watching what actually spawns within 6 blocks for 5 seconds, so it works for vanilla and modded effects alike, with counts for each. Particles that mods spawn without a registered type are listed by class name so they can still be hidden. /particles scan starts one from chat.

Block-specific particles

Hide the breaking, mining and block-dust (sprinting and landing) particles of a single block ID, every block from a mod (modid:*), or a block tag (#c:ores). A keybind toggles the block you're looking at.

Global hiding

Hide a particle across the whole game with one switch, or type its ID on the Hidden page if you already know it. The Hidden page lists every rule and where it applies.

Active rules and Hold to show all

Apply All rules, Everywhere only, Zones only, or None (show all), from the menu or a keybind; your rules are kept either way. A separate key pauses every rule only while held, for a quick before/after look.

Compatibility and limitations

Download the jar that matches your Minecraft version and loader. Both builds have the same features.

  • Client-side only. Install it on your own game. Servers don't need it, and the commands send nothing to the server.
  • Works alongside particle optimisation mods such as Particle Core and Sodium (tested on 1.21.1).
  • Not available for Fabric or for Minecraft versions other than the two above.

Limitations

  • Custom renderers: effects a mod draws with its own renderer, rather than Minecraft's particle system, can't be filtered.
  • Limited testing: tested so far on one large modpack (All the Mods 10: To the Sky) and a minimal development setup. Expect rough edges with other mods, and please report bugs.
  • Zones and server addresses: zones are tied to the address you connect with; connecting through a different address won't show them.
  • Performance varies: hiding particles saves the work of creating and drawing them, but whether that's noticeable depends on how many there are and what else limits your frame rate.

Frequently asked questions

How do I disable specific particles in Minecraft?

Vanilla Minecraft only has one Particles setting (All, Decreased, Minimal) that applies to every particle. To turn off particular ones, you need a mod. With Particle Filter, run /particles, scan near the effect, and switch on Everywhere next to the particle you want gone. If you already know its ID, type it on the Hidden page instead.

Can I hide particles only inside my base?

Yes, that's what zones are for. Switch on Here after a scan, or create a zone on the Zones page, then size it to cover your base, farm or room. Particles in that zone's list are hidden inside the box and appear normally outside it.

How do I find out what a modded particle is called?

Use the scanner. It records every particle spawned within 6 blocks of where you're looking for 5 seconds and lists them by ID, for example ae2:energy_fx, with a count for each. The part before the colon is usually the mod that adds it. Particles spawned without a registered type are listed by their class name.

Can I disable block-breaking particles for specific blocks?

Yes. Particle Filter can hide the breaking, mining and block-dust particles of a single block, all blocks from one mod (modid:*), or a block tag such as #c:ores. There's also a keybind that toggles this for the block you're looking at.

Do I need to install it on the server?

No. Particle Filter is client-side only: put it in your own mods folder and it works in singleplayer and on servers that don't have it. Other players aren't affected by your rules.

How is it different from Sodium Extra or Particle Core?

Those mods let you switch particle types off across the whole game, and they do that well. Particle Filter focuses on two other things: a scanner that tells you which particle you're looking at, and rules that only apply inside zones or to specific blocks' breaking effects.

Particle Filter has been tested alongside Particle Core and Sodium on 1.21.1. It hasn't been specifically tested with Sodium Extra.

Does hiding particles improve FPS?

It can, but it depends on your situation. Filtering happens in the particle engine, so hidden particles are never created and don't cost anything to update or draw. If thousands of particles are spawning around you, that can make a real difference. If your frame rate is limited by something else, such as chunk rendering, entities or your graphics card, you may not notice much. There are no official benchmarks; try it with your own setup.

Can I see the particles again later?

Yes. Unhide any rule from the Hidden page, set Active rules to None (show all), or hold the Hold to Show All Particles key. Your rules are saved in config/particlefilter.json either way.
